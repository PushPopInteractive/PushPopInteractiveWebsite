Peoples & Cities

Who Lives Here

Sixteen peoples share Alrathel. Twelve settlements anchor them. Three powers hold the peace between them, and everyone describes that peace with the same two words.

The three powers

Allegiance here describes governments and broad peoples. It does not dictate what any individual believes, worships, or does for a living.

The Kingdom of Eldrath

Human-led, and the largest power in the world by a wide margin — humans are by far the most numerous people in Alrathel. The kingdom is an alliance of sovereign states, not an empire: the High Elves of Elandril and the Dwarves of Deepforge stand with it as equals, and neither is a subject of the human crown.

Humans and High Elves generally worship Aurelia. The Dwarves are fiercely devoted to Hefen, and consider the question closed.

The Sacred Grove

The loosest of the three — a coalition of nature-loving peoples rather than a centralised state. Its members are the Wood Elves and a broad beastkin grouping that takes in the Beastkin, Kemono, Draka and Lizardkin. That is a political umbrella, not an erasure: they remain entirely distinct peoples who happen to agree about something important.

The Sacred Grove is a name, not a place. The wood elves do keep real sacred forests, and their beastkin friends are freely welcome in them. Members of the other two powers, as a rule, are not.

Its peoples are largely followers of Vivili.

The Shadow Dominion

Led by the dark elves of Val'thar, deep in the Crystal Wilds. Orc and goblinoid peoples live within it as vassals, and their vassalage is oppressive — an orc vassal village stands at the foot of Val'thar's castle, and nobody pretends it is there by choice.

Dark elves are not the cartoon evil that some tellings make of them. They are haughty, they consider themselves the natural first among peoples, and they rule their subjects without mercy. Those are three different things and the distinction matters if you plan to do business there.

The Nocturne of The Light Below joined the Dominion as uneasy allies, never vassals — a relationship built on underground proximity, practical survival, and one other reason that surprises outsiders every time they hear it.

The sixteen peoples

These are the peoples of Alrathel. They are not what a Returner arrives as — see the three vessels for that — but they are who you will spend your time among, work for, argue with, and be judged by.

Dhampir, Constructs and Amalgams hold no race-wide allegiance to any of the three powers; individuals hold whatever loyalties they like. The Aurelians belong to none of the three and consider themselves above the question. Half-Elves and Half-Orcs live in human cities without that settling anything about who they answer to.

How the peoples look

Dwarves

Dwarves are built to their own plan entirely, men and women both. A dwarf man stands about 1.60 m and a dwarf woman about 1.40 m — short beside a Returner, and built wide enough that the height stops being the thing you notice.

The elven peoples

The four elven peoples share one build. What tells them apart is colouring, and it is remarkably consistent:

Half-Elves are the deliberate exception to every rule on this table. Lilia, the guild receptionist who registers every new Returner in the world, has bright green hair — and that is not an oddity, it is simply the range.

The Nocturne

Blue skin, small horns, and a village underground. Because of how they look, the wider world sometimes takes the Nocturne for demon-kind. They are not. They are a mortal people, and the mistake is old, tiresome, and made constantly.

What genuinely puzzles outsiders is their faith. The Nocturne are all fiercely devoted to Aurelia, goddess of Light — a twilight people whose great weakness is direct bright daylight, worshipping the sun's own goddess with more fervour than anyone else alive. Nobody has satisfactorily explained it, including the Nocturne, who regard the question as slightly rude.

Their alliance with the dark elves runs on more than proximity. The Nocturne consider it a sacred duty: they evangelise among the dark elves, trying to turn them from cold and selfish habits toward Aurelia's light. It is the reason the alliance has endured.

It is also going both ways. Roughly as many Nocturne have been drawn toward dark elf society as dark elves have been drawn toward Aurelia — which the devout among them experience as a genuine and continuing grief. Their mission makes converts and costs them their own.

The dark elves, for their part, respect Nocturne strength and are quietly unnerved by their bond with the goddess. That unease is most of what keeps the Nocturne allies rather than vassals.

The Nocturne themselves read their centuries of hardship as being tested. Whether anyone is testing them is not a question the world can answer.

The Aurelians

A people in a cloud city, bearing a goddess's name, who are markedly less devout than almost anyone else — and considerably less devout than the Nocturne, who have never had a city in the sky.

Aurelian culture runs on the general conviction that they are above it all, including the rituals of devotion to a higher power. They see every other people as beneath them, and they say so with a politeness that makes it worse.

They tell the world they evolved past needing wings. Whatever else is true, no Aurelian alive has them.

The asymmetry is not lost on anyone: the arrogant namesakes have never visibly been punished for it, while the fiercely faithful Nocturne have endured hardship after hardship. Priests have several answers. None of them satisfies a Nocturne.

Getting along

Alrathel is not a grim world and this is not a grim page. With monsters, dragons and slimes abroad, humanoids close ranks with humanoids, and anything remotely humanoid counts as close enough. Deep exclusion between peoples is not believable in these times, and it is not what you will find.

Old custom exists. Individual rudeness exists. A village that has never seen a Construct will stare. What does not exist is institutional exclusion: nobody is refused a bed, a job, or a seat at a table because of the body they are walking around in.

The live suspicion in this world points at Returners, not at appearances. Strange bodies are ordinary here. People who arrived from somewhere else, and might leave again, are not.
