World History

A Thousand Years of Quiet

Alrathel has spent ten centuries not being destroyed, and has spent most of them agreeing not to examine the reason too closely.

The Demon King

Roughly a thousand years ago, the world came very close to ending.

The Demon King was not a strong monster with a crown on it. He was a world-ending force — the kind of power the word king is far too domestic for. Every account that survives agrees on the scale, and the accounts agree on very little else.

One fact is preserved everywhere, in every telling, and is the only piece of practical knowledge the age left behind: he needs a body. Whatever he is without one, it is not enough to walk the world. To be truly here, he must be housed.

He was stopped. He was not killed — nobody who has read the old tomes carefully will tell you he was killed — and the difference has kept scholars awake for ten centuries.

The hero who is not agreed upon

A hero ended that war. Beyond the fact of it, the record thins out alarmingly fast.

What survives is broad description in old tomes, and stories passed down through so many generations that they have worn smooth. Nothing has a face. Nothing has a home town. Two accounts of the same battle will disagree about which season it happened in.

The hero is the most prominent person in history who might have been a Returner. Might. It is impossible to establish what people at the time believed they were looking at, and the surviving texts contradict one another in ways that are difficult to explain by ordinary carelessness.

Selvara En, the royal archivist at Eldrath, has spent years on precisely this question and has not resolved it. She is candid about that. She is less candid about why she keeps going.

Three copies of the same chronicle. Two of them are missing the same page. I am told this happens. Selvara En, royal archivist

What demons actually are

The war left the world with a word it uses badly.

Demons come in a great variety of shapes, and they are as morally varied as anyone else. Some are misunderstood. Some are the worst thing you will ever meet. Most are somewhere in the middle, which is exactly the distribution you would get from any people.

Being demon-kind establishes no allegiance whatsoever — not to the Demon King, not to any god, not to evil. A demon who presents as an ordinary person generally receives the ordinary tolerance the world extends to anyone unusual. Nella, the succubus who runs a stall at the Great Desert night market, is the example everybody reaches for, largely because she is very good at what she does and has been there for years.

Clerics can sense demons nearby. So can a trained white dove. Neither tells you anything about the demon except that it is there — which is information, not a verdict.

The long peace and the three powers

Out of ten centuries without a world-ending war, three great powers settled into the shape they hold today.

Kingdom of Eldrath

Human-led alliance

Humans, with the High Elves of Elandril and the Dwarves of Deepforge as sovereign allies — not subjects. Neither elf nor dwarf answers to the human crown, and both would like that understood.

The Sacred Grove

Coalition

The loosest of the three: wood elves and a broad beastkin grouping, bound by shared reverence rather than a single government. Not one place — though the wood elves do keep real sacred forests, and are particular about who walks in them.

The Shadow Dominion

Dominion

Led by the dark elves of Val'thar, with orc and goblinoid peoples held as vassals under a rule that is frankly oppressive. The Nocturne stand alongside them as uneasy allies rather than subjects.

All three are, at this moment, at peace — for now. Everybody uses those last two words. Nobody has said what would break it, when, or which way the first blow would fall.

The court at Eldrath

King Wilhelm sits the throne in the royal palace at Eldrath: a knight before he was a king, and still built like one. Queen Halwen Cor rules beside him. Edric Vaun keeps the household as royal chamberlain, and Captain Maelis Rook commands the palace guard.

The capital's embassy district holds eight foreign delegations — every people with a state of its own is represented, and the ones without a state are represented by the fact that they live here already.

The wave

For most of those ten centuries, a Returner was a rarity — one in a generation, and often not that. They belong to legend the way comets do: real, documented, and not expected in your lifetime.

That has changed. Returners now arrive constantly. They walk into the Adventurer's Guild in Eldrath in a steady stream, register at the counter, and go out into a world that has no framework for this many of them at once.

Ordinary people are alarmed, and it would be dishonest to pretend the alarm is unreasonable. It is not about how Returners look — Alrathel is full of unusual bodies and has been for as long as anyone can remember. It is that a Returner came from somewhere else, and might, for all anyone knows, be able to leave again.

The prejudice is real and it is aimed there. It is also, importantly, not the kind that closes doors. No innkeeper in the capital turns away an Amalgam. No baker refuses to hire a Construct. What you get is the awkward question, the pause before the answer, the person at the next table who moves one seat down.

Places are crossing too

Whatever brings the Returners, it is not only bringing people. Fragments of other worlds have started turning up in the countryside — a diner, a platform, a ward — intact, wrong, and entirely unexplained. See Places that do not belong.

What nobody knows

This is the honest end of the chronicle, and it is short.

  • Why Returners come. No god has claimed it. No temple can demonstrate it. The popular answer — that Aurelia sends them ahead of a coming darkness — is a belief, and is treated as one by everybody who has actually looked into it.
  • Who or what opens the way. Unknown. The obvious suspect is the obvious suspect for a reason, and that is as far as the evidence goes.
  • Whether the millennium hero was one of them. The record will not say, and the shape of the gaps in the record is itself a thing some archivists have started to find interesting.

Alrathel is a world that has been at peace long enough to have forgotten how the peace was bought. It is now filling up with people who were not born here at all, and every power in the world is watching to see what that does.
