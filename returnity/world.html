The World

Alrathel

One world, shared by everyone who plays. Seventeen regions in three layers: a surface that loops back on itself, an Underworld running beneath it, and a city in the clouds that no road has ever reached.

A chart of Alrathel

Every line on this chart is a border you can walk across. Two regions drawn near each other with no line between them do not touch — the line is the only thing that means anything. Level ranges are printed under each name.

A world that radiates

Difficulty in Alrathel is not a line drawn from one edge of the map to the other. It is a ring. Eldrath, the human capital, sits in the centre, and everything gets rougher the further out you go — in every direction equally.

Two things decide how hard a place is, and only two:

  • Whether anybody lives there. A region with a settlement in it is patrolled, traded through, and walked. A region with none is not.
  • How far it is from Eldrath. The capital is the centre of civilisation and the reach of its roads is finite.

These stack, and the second can beat the first. Winterhold is a real city with a real garrison — but it is in the far north, so the tundra around it is far harder country than the woodlands around Sylvanor, which sit one step from the capital gates.

Five regions have no settlement of any kind: the Rugged Highlands, the Deep Jungle, the Badlands, the Volcanic Lands and the Icebound Wastes. The Highlands share a border with the capital's own region and are still more dangerous than places twice as far out, for exactly that reason.

Reading the level ranges

Every region below carries a level range. They overlap on purpose: a Returner leaving a region at the top of its range arrives in the next one able to survive it, not to dominate it. Returners advance to level 50. Only the last two surface regions and the sky city hold anything above that, and only their rarest and largest creatures.

The surface

Thirteen regions, connected in loops rather than a single corridor. You can walk from any one to any other without ever retracing your steps, if you are willing to take the long way around.

The Underworld

Three regions beneath the surface, entered from the Deep Woodlands, the Wetlands, the Rugged Highlands, the Badlands and the Volcanic Lands. The Underworld is not a dungeon — it is a second layer of the world, with its own cities, its own weather of a kind, and its own road network. It also connects distant surface regions to one another far faster than walking around the top would.

Being underground is itself a form of remoteness. The Underworld runs harder than a surface region the same distance out.

RegionLevelsSettlementWhat it is
Fungal Wilds32–40The Light BelowFungi as food, shelter, communication network and predator, all at once. The Nocturne make their home here, in the only village in the world lit from underneath.
Underdeep35–43DeepforgeThe central travel layer, and the dwarven undermountain city. Vibration hunters and burrowers own the dark between the halls. The dwarves live in it; that does not make it safe.
Crystal Wilds38–46Val'tharThe deepest inhabited place in the world. Living crystal, reflected light and resonance shape everything down here, including the dark elf city that rules it.

Getting around

The map is yours alone

A new Returner knows nothing about this world. The map fills in only where that character has personally been, or where somebody reliable has told them what is there — a bought map, a treasure chart, a person who owes you an answer. Nothing is shared automatically: not with your other characters, not with the party you are standing next to. Their maps are their own business.

Wagons

The overland wagon network is the ordinary way to cross settled country. Three rules govern it, and they are worth learning before you need them:

  • You can only buy passage to a stop you already know. Somebody has to walk it the first time. That somebody is you.
  • Wagons stop running after dark on any road that is not settled heartland. Drivers who refuse a road at night are not being superstitious.
  • One region to the next is about half a minute. Every further region adds another half-minute to the ride.

Tunnel Runners

The Underworld has its own service, and it is not a wagon. Tunnel Runners are large, intimidating, and — around anything humanoid — remarkably docile underground animals. Teams of them are kept at travel halls and carry passengers along the real, connected passages of the deep: from The Light Below to Deepforge, and from Deepforge onward to Val'thar.

Individual Runners can be trained as personal mounts by those with the patience for it.

Places that do not belong

Something in this world tears pieces off other worlds and drops them here.

They arrive whole and wrong: a roadside diner, a rail platform, a hospital ward, standing in country that has never built anything like them. They keep their own materials, their own light, their own scale. Nothing about them blends in, and that is the point.

No one in Alrathel can explain them. There is no scholar with the answer, no temple with a doctrine, no elder in a village who remembers when it appeared. They are rare, and they are worth going out of your way for.

One region has no parent and no land border at all, and is made entirely of them. It is reached only through unstable crossings that surface somewhere else and belong to none of the places they open in.
